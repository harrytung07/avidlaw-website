**Understanding the Corporate Veil**

One of the greatest advantages of incorporating a business is the
limited liability protection it provides. Incorporation creates a
separate legal entity, meaning the corporation — not its owners — is
responsible for debts and legal obligations.

This protection is known as the corporate veil.

**Legal Foundation**

The principle originates from the famous case *Salomon v. Salomon*
(1897), where the court held that once incorporated, a company is a
distinct legal person separate from its shareholders.

“The company is at law a different person altogether … and shareholders
are not liable beyond their investment.”  
— *Salomon v. Salomon*, 1897

This case forms the cornerstone of modern corporate law.

**How the Corporate Veil Protects Owners**

Consider *Summit Ridge Construction Ltd.*, a construction company.

If the company borrows money, enters contracts, and later faces
financial losses, creditors may pursue company assets only—not the
personal home, savings, or property of shareholders (unless they
personally guaranteed the debt).

This protection encourages business growth and entrepreneurship.

**Myth vs Reality**

**Myth:**

“If I incorporate, I can never be personally liable.”

**Reality:**

Courts may pierce the corporate veil and hold individuals personally
liable if the corporation is misused.

Situations where owners may still be personally liable:

- Fraud or intentional wrongdoing

- Personal guarantees on loans

- Mixing personal and corporate funds

- Using a corporation to avoid legal obligations

- Serious misconduct or bad faith

**When Courts Pierce the Veil**

Courts lift the corporate veil only in exceptional cases to prevent
injustice.

| **Grounds** | **Explanation** |

|----|----|

| Company formed for wrongdoing | Business created to defraud |

| Wrongful acts through corporation | Directors instruct illegal conduct |

| Corporation is alter ego/sham | No separation between personal & corporate finances |

| Statutory liability | Certain laws impose personal liability |


**Case Insight: *SPC Holdings v. Gabriel***

In this case, a roofing company dissolved after being sued and
transferred assets to a new company to avoid paying the judgment. The
court pierced the veil and held the directors personally liable.

**Key message:**  
The corporate veil protects honest business owners — not those who
misuse the corporate form.

**Best Practices: Protecting Your Corporate Shield**

To maintain limited liability protection, business owners should:

- Keep business and personal finances separate

- Maintain proper corporate records

- Avoid unnecessary personal guarantees

- Act in good faith and follow the law

- Seek legal advice before major transactions

**Conclusion**

- Incorporation offers powerful personal asset protection

- But protection is not absolute — misuse can result in personal
  liability

- When used properly, incorporation is one of the most effective tools
  for business growth and risk management
