**Incorporating a Company in British Columbia: A Practical Guide**

Starting a business is an exciting milestone. Incorporation provides
important legal and financial advantages by creating a separate legal
entity that can own property, enter into contracts, and limit personal
liability.

This guide outlines key steps and considerations when incorporating a
company in British Columbia.

**1. Name Search & Reservation**

Before incorporating, the proposed company name must be approved by the
BC Registrar of Companies.

A valid company name typically includes:

| Term                  | Meaning                    |

|-----------------------|----------------------------|

| Distinctive element   | Brand or unique identifier |

| Descriptive element   | Nature of the business     |

| Corporate designation | Ltd., Inc., Corp., etc.    |


Example:  
*Chilly Bliss Frozen Treats Inc.*

You may also choose a numbered company (e.g., 1234567 B.C. Ltd.) without
name approval.

Name reservations are valid for 56 days.

**2. Prepare Corporate Articles**

Corporate articles set out the internal governance rules, including:

- Rights and duties of directors and shareholders

- Meeting and voting procedures

- Share restrictions

Most small private companies may adopt *Table 1 Standard Articles* under
BC regulation.

**3. Sign an Incorporation Agreement**

Each incorporator must sign an agreement confirming:

- Intention to form a company

- Number and class of shares to be taken

This agreement becomes part of the company’s permanent records.

**4. File the Incorporation Application**

The incorporation filing is submitted online through BC Registry
Services.

Once approved, you will receive:

- Certificate of Incorporation

- Notice of Articles

- Certified Incorporation Application

These documents are evidence of corporate existence and must be kept in
the corporate records book.

**5. Set Up Registered & Records Office**

All BC companies must maintain a registered office and records office in
BC.

Company records include:

- Certificate of Incorporation

- Central Securities Register

- Register of Directors

- Minutes and resolutions

- Share certificates

Proper record-keeping is legally required and essential for corporate
governance.

**Directors & Share Structure**

**Directors**

Directors manage the company’s business and affairs.  
Certain individuals cannot act as directors (e.g., minors, undischarged
bankrupts).

**Share Structure**

Shares typically carry:

- Voting rights

- Dividend rights

- Liquidation rights

Multiple share classes may be issued for tax planning, financing, and
control arrangements.
