**Understanding Business Acquisitions: What Buyers and Sellers Need to
Know**

Buying or selling a business is one of the most significant transactions
an entrepreneur or company will undertake. Whether the deal involves a
small family business or a large corporate operation, the legal
considerations can be complex. At Avid Law Corporation, we assist
clients through every stage of the process to ensure their interests are
protected and the transaction proceeds smoothly.

**Share Purchase vs. Asset Purchase**

Most business acquisitions take one of two forms:  
(1) a share purchase, where the buyer acquires the shares of the
company, or  
(2) an asset purchase, where the buyer acquires specific assets and
sometimes specific liabilities.

Each structure has advantages and disadvantages involving tax,
liability, and operational considerations. For example:

- Share purchases are often simpler and faster, as the corporate entity
  continues without interruption.

- Asset purchases allow buyers to “pick and choose” the assets they want
  and avoid many unknown liabilities.

Choosing the right structure requires careful legal and accounting
advice.

**The Lawyer’s Role in a Business Purchase**

Legal counsel plays a critical role in business acquisitions. Our
responsibilities typically include:

- Understanding the client’s commercial goals

- Reviewing or preparing letters of intent or term sheets

- Conducting due diligence to identify risks

- Negotiating the purchase agreement

- Protecting the client through warranties, representations, and
  indemnities

- Coordinating closing documents and financing arrangements

- Advising on regulatory requirements and consents

Engaging a lawyer early can prevent costly mistakes and ensure the
transaction is structured to your benefit.

**Letters of Intent (LOI)**

Many transactions begin with a letter of intent or term sheet. While
usually non-binding on the business terms, an LOI serves as an important
roadmap by:

- Identifying key deal terms

- Highlighting areas that require negotiation

- Establishing a framework for drafting the final agreement

- Setting out binding provisions such as confidentiality, exclusivity,
  and deadlines

A properly drafted LOI can streamline the negotiations and help avoid
future disputes.

**Key Elements of a Purchase Agreement**

Whether buying shares or assets, most purchase agreements include:

- A clear description of what is being sold

- The purchase price and how it is allocated

- Adjustments to price based on working capital

- Vendor representations and warranties

- Indemnities for breaches or unknown liabilities

- Conditions that must be met before closing

- Closing documents and post-closing obligations

Buyers often rely heavily on representations, warranties, and
indemnities because these protect against risks that cannot be
discovered during due diligence.

**Regulatory and Third-Party Approvals**

Some transactions require additional approvals or filings, including:

- Competition Act notifications for large transactions

- Investment Canada Act reviews for foreign purchasers

- Industry-specific regulatory consents

- Bank or landlord consents

- Assignment approvals for key contracts or licences

Missing a required consent can delay the deal or even trigger breaches
of important agreements. We work closely with clients to ensure all
approvals are obtained.

**Closing the Deal**

Closing a business acquisition involves coordinating:

- Corporate resolutions and approvals

- Transfer documents (shares, contracts, assets, intellectual property,
  etc.)

- Payment and financing logistics

- Updating corporate records post-closing

- Employment arrangements for staff being retained

- Non-competition, confidentiality, or consulting agreements

A detailed closing agenda helps all parties stay organized and ensures
that nothing is overlooked.

**When Transactions Break Down**

Sometimes, deals fall apart due to issues such as:

- Misrepresentations or inaccurate warranties

- Failure to satisfy conditions

- Repudiation (where one party indicates it no longer intends to be
  bound)

In these situations, parties must act promptly and strategically. Our
firm assists clients in asserting or defending their rights—including
preserving evidence, evaluating remedies, and pursuing or resisting
claims.
