### 1. Regarding Children

**Misconception:** Believing you have no financial obligation to a partner's children from a previous relationship.

**Reality:** Under B.C.'s Family Law Act, if you act as a parent to your partner's child during your relationship, you may be required to pay child support upon separation.

### 2. Increase in Property Value

**Misconception:** Assuming that pre-relationship property and any growth in its value will automatically remain separate.

**Reality:** In B.C., the increase in the value of an asset that occurs during the relationship is presumptively considered family property.

### 3. Foreign Assets

**Misconception:** Thinking that assets located outside of Canada do not need to be disclosed.

**Reality:** Full and honest financial disclosure is the foundation of a valid agreement. Concealing overseas assets may cause the court to set aside the entire agreement.

### 4. Income and Future Changes

**Misconception:** Believing that current income does not need to be disclosed because it is subject to change.

**Reality:** A complete and accurate picture of your financial circumstances at the time of signing, including current income, must be disclosed.

### 5. Waiver of Spousal Support

**Misconception:** Believing that signing a spousal support waiver is a "100% risk-free" guarantee.

**Reality:** A court may still intervene if a significant change in circumstances makes the waiver significantly unfair in the future.

### 6. The Family Home

**Misconception:** Thinking that if a property is registered only in one partner's name, it cannot be considered the "family home."

**Reality:** In B.C., a property that is ordinarily occupied by the couple is treated as the family home, regardless of whose name appears on title.

### 7. Gifts and Inheritances

**Misconception:** Assuming that personal gifts or inheritances do not need to be addressed in the agreement.

**Reality:** While generally excluded property, they can be converted into family property if commingled into joint accounts or used to purchase joint assets.

### 8. Enforceability and Fairness of the Agreement

**Misconception:** Thinking that once an agreement is signed, it is "ironclad" and cannot be challenged in any circumstance.

**Reality:** A court can set aside or modify an agreement if it was procured unfairly, such as through a lack of independent legal advice, inadequate financial disclosure, or under duress.
